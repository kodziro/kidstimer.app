Best Visual Timer Apps for Kids (2026 Comparison)

By Natalia Jakubczyk-Gajewska · Last updated: August 4, 2026

There is no single best visual timer for every child — the right pick depends on your child's age, what motivates them, and whether you're on iPhone or Android. For toddlers and picture-motivated kids on iOS, our own Visual Timer for Kids is the strongest fit; for brand familiarity and cross-device syncing, Time Timer leads; for screen-free homes, a physical Time Timer still beats any app. Below is an honest comparison of the six options we'd genuinely recommend — including where each one, ours included, falls short.

A quick note on trust: we make one of the apps on this list. To keep the comparison useful, every rating and price below was checked on the live App Store or Google Play listing, competitors' strengths are stated as plainly as our own, and criticisms of other apps come from their own users' reviews, not from us.

Quick answer: best by situation

  • Best for toddlers & picture motivation: Visual Timer for Kids (ours, iOS)
  • Best-known brand, Apple Watch & cross-device: Time Timer
  • Best screen-free option: Time Timer physical timer
  • Best budget iOS pick: Visual Countdown Timer
  • Best full morning-routine app: Happy Kids Timer Chores
  • Best free Android option: Visual Timer – Countdown (Humane Digital)

Visual Timer for Kids (our app)

Our app takes a different approach from the classic disappearing disk: as the countdown runs, a picture is slowly revealed, so the timer gives your child something to look forward to rather than just something to watch shrink. There are 200 built-in pictures, 25 backgrounds, 20 music themes and 10 time's-up sounds, and you can use your own photos — a picture of the family dog is a surprisingly powerful reason to keep brushing. The timer works in the background and notifies you when time is up, and it's translated into 14 languages.

Honest limitations: it's iOS-only — there is no Android version, so Android families should look at Visual Timer – Countdown below. The free version includes a limited selection of pictures and sounds; the full library requires the premium in-app purchase. And the picture-reveal format is aimed squarely at roughly ages 2–8 — teens and adults will likely prefer a plainer timer.

Fits best: toddlers, preschoolers and early-elementary kids — especially children with ADHD or autism who respond to a visible, motivating countdown for routines like screen time, tooth brushing and tidy-up.

Time Timer (app)

Time Timer is the app version of the famous red-disk classroom timer, made by the same company that has built visual timers for over 30 years. It's the most complete ecosystem here: iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch and Android, with multiple simultaneous timers, and routine sequencing and cross-device syncing in the paid unlock ($9.95). If your child already knows the red disk from school, the familiarity transfers instantly.

Limitations reported by users: some long-time users say in recent App Store reviews that features they previously had for free have moved behind the paid unlock, and one paying subscriber reported a months-old crash bug in the timer-lists feature. The Android version is rated notably lower (3.8★) than the iOS one. The disk visual is also more abstract than a picture — some young children watch it; many don't.

Fits best: families and classrooms already invested in Time Timer, older kids, and anyone who wants the same timer on a watch, phone and desktop.

Visual Countdown Timer

Visual Countdown Timer by Fehners Software is the volume leader on iOS — over 40,000 ratings at 4.6★ — and a long-time favorite of special-education teachers. It runs green-amber-red as time passes and shows a reward picture at the end, and the paid tier is the cheapest way into a full-featured kids' timer: $3.99 for premium or $1.99 just to remove ads.

Limitations reported by users: the free version is ad-supported, and several recent App Store reviews — including from a pediatric occupational therapist and a kindergarten teacher — report inappropriate or scam ads appearing right after the timer finishes, on screens children were watching. Reviewers also note the app must stay open to run and that times are set in 10-second increments. If you use it with kids, budget the $1.99 ad removal from day one.

Fits best: budget-conscious families and classrooms on iOS or Android, with the paid upgrade.

Little Timer Hatch Countdown

Little Timer Hatch Countdown has one of the sweetest concepts on this list: an egg slowly hatches as time runs, revealing an animated animal at the end. It's simple, well-loved (4.5★ from 14,591 ratings) and the premium unlock is only $2.99.

Limitations: the App Store listing shows it was last updated in 2021, so it hasn't seen new features or fixes in years. Reviewers' most common request is a child lock — the on-screen buttons remain tappable while the timer runs, and parents report kids pausing the countdown mid-wait. Most animals and sounds sit behind the premium purchase.

Fits best: toddlers and short waits — turn-taking, tidy-up, waiting for dinner — where charm matters more than features.

Happy Kids Timer Chores

Happy Kids Timer Chores is less a timer than a full routine coach: animated characters walk your child through getting dressed, brushing teeth and packing up, each step on its own countdown, with stars as rewards. With over a million Android installs it's the biggest cross-platform option here, and parents of kids with ADHD frequently credit it with independent mornings.

Limitations reported by users: several recent App Store reviews report the app crashing or freezing, with emails to the developer going unanswered. Reviewers have also criticized the setup for forcing a boy/girl choice with gendered colors. And because it's built around fixed routines, it's more to manage than a simple timer — overkill if all you want is a visible five minutes.

Fits best: families on either platform who want the entire morning or bedtime routine gamified, not just timed.

Visual Timer – Countdown (Android)

If you're on Android, Visual Timer – Countdown by Humane Digital is the easy recommendation: free with no in-app purchases we could find, rated 4.8★ by nearly 25,000 users, with over a million installs. It's a clean shrinking-disk timer rather than a kids' app — no pictures, characters or rewards — but it does the core job of making time visible, and it costs nothing.

Fits best: Android households — and we say that as an iOS-only app. It's what we'd install on a grandparent's Android tablet.

App vs. physical timer: when a device wins

A physical Time Timer (Original, PLUS, MOD and other models, from about $25) makes more sense than any app in two situations. First, screen-free households: a device on the shelf involves no phone, no notifications and no ads, ever. Second, classrooms: it's always visible at the front of the room, can't be paused by little fingers, and never runs out of battery mid-lesson. The trade-offs are price — one device costs more than every app upgrade on this list combined — and flexibility: no pictures, no music, and each model has a fixed maximum duration. Many families end up with both: a device for the classroom or kitchen shelf, an app for everywhere else.

Frequently asked questions

Is Time Timer or Visual Timer for Kids better?

Time Timer is better if you want the best-known brand, Apple Watch support and syncing across devices; Visual Timer for Kids is better for young children who are motivated by pictures rather than a shrinking disk. Time Timer shows time draining away, which suits older kids and adults. Visual Timer for Kids reveals a picture as time passes, which gives toddlers and preschoolers a reason to keep watching. Many families use one or the other purely based on which visual clicks with their child.

What is the best visual timer app for kids with ADHD?

Any timer that keeps remaining time constantly visible can help a child with ADHD — the visibility matters more than the brand. Time Timer's disk and Visual Timer for Kids' picture reveal both make time concrete, which is what children with time blindness need. Pick the one whose visual your child actually wants to look at, and keep the device in their line of sight. Our ADHD timer guide covers the setup routine in detail.

Is there a good free visual timer for kids?

Yes — on iPhone and iPad, Visual Timer for Kids is free to download and use, with optional premium pictures and music via in-app purchase. On Android, Visual Timer – Countdown by Humane Digital is free with no in-app purchases. Most other kids' timers are free with ads or a limited selection until you pay a small unlock fee.

Should I get a timer app or a physical visual timer?

Choose a physical timer for screen-free households and classrooms, and an app when you want pictures, sounds and flexible durations for less money. A physical Time Timer never shows an ad and is always visible on the shelf, but costs from about $25 and does one thing. An app on a phone or iPad you already own is cheaper, more customizable, and rewards the child at the end.
